What Resolve changes

Level 2 · in the app

Safe cases never become tickets

The user asks in the app; the agent reads the live Adapty profile, explains the charge, gives the store steps, and grants free days within limits checked in code: max days, one grant per user per 30 days, paying-now users only, never lifetime. Refunds, threats and “I want a human” always hand off.

  • Calm got 42% of subscription questions to self-serve with static answers; this answers from the profile.
  • Eval: 7 of 8 scripted chats right; one model call per message.
Level 1 · for the person

7 minutes → one click

Every handoff and piece of feedback arrives triaged: cause, 2–3 options with different actions, a ready reply, risk, and the profile lines used as evidence. Accept, edit or reject; only then anything runs in Adapty.

  • Eval, 32 cases incl. 12 adversarial: category 31/32, first-option action 28/32, evidence lines grounded 230/238.
  • No refund is ever issued by an agent; the store refunds.
Money

Refunds avoided, paywall fixed

  • Refund asks turned into 7 days of access at 30–50%: $1.0–1.6k a month back on FitBuddy, ~1.4% of gross.
  • Billing failures are a third of Android cancellations: a reply that links to the card fix recovers a share.
  • The part that scales with revenue: 12 of 16 trial complaints came from one onboarding flow → a draft A/B test in Adapty. The paywall gets fixed instead of the rating.

Assumptions we own:
  • “One in four events becomes a ticket” and “30–50% of refund asks accept 7 days” are ours; Refund Saver's 40–50% is the ceiling we borrow. Nobody publishes ticket volumes for subscription apps; Calm is the one public case.
  • Refund rate is not ticket rate: most refunds go straight to Apple or Google. The paywall loop does not depend on that premise.
  • Review studies classify recent reviews of large apps by keyword or model; they are not store-wide rates. Benchmark reports are vendor data; Refund Saver's figures are Adapty's marketing.
